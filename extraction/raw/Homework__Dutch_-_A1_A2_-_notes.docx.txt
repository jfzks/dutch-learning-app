1. Slagen (Personal Success)
You use slagen when a person achieves a goal through their own effort, skill, or persistence. It is almost always paired with the preposition in or used when talking about passing an exam.
Subject: A person (I, you, he, she).
Focus: Achievement and effort.
Preposition: Slagen in (to succeed in) or Slagen voor (to pass an exam).
Examples:
Ik ben geslaagd voor mijn rijexamen. (I passed my driving test.)
Zij slaagde erin de berg te beklimmen. (She succeeded in climbing the mountain.)
2. Lukken (Situational Success)
You use lukken when a task or event goes well. In this case, the thing being done is the subject of the sentence, not the person. If you want to mention the person, you use a dative structure (it succeeds to me).
Subject: A thing, a task, or the impersonal "het" (it).
Focus: The outcome or the fact that it worked out.
Structure: Het lukt (iemand). (It works out for someone.)
Examples:
Het lukt me niet om deze pot te openen. (I’m not succeeding in opening this jar / It isn't working for me.)
De taart is goed gelukt. (The cake turned out well / succeeded.)
